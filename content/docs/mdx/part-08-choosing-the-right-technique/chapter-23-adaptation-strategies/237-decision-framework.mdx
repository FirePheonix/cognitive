# 23.7 Decision Framework

This section covers the details of 23.7 Decision Framework.

Stop at the first step that moves the eval. Each later step costs more to build and more to undo.

![Adaptation ladder: prompt, in-context examples, RAG, LoRA, full fine-tune](/part-08/chapter-23/adaptation-ladder.png)
*Fig: Prompt and examples are hours. RAG is an index. LoRA is a small training run. Full fine-tuning is the one you have to repeat when the base model is replaced.*

Ask four questions, in order.

1. Does a clearer instruction or five examples fix it on the golden set? Stay in the prompt (23.1, 23.2).
2. Is the miss a missing or stale fact? Retrieve it (23.3). If recall is already high and the model ignores the passage, the bug is grounding, not the index.
3. Is the miss a stable behavior — format, tone, tool style — that survives a good prompt? Train an adapter (23.5).
4. Is the capability absent from the base model, with thousands of consistent demonstrations? Full fine-tuning (23.4), or a bigger base, which is often cheaper than a bad fine-tune.

Write the answer down next to the eval delta. “We fine-tuned” is not a result. “The prompt scored 61, retrieval scored 84, LoRA scored 86, and we shipped retrieval” is.
