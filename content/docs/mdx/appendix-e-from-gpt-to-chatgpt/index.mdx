# Appendix E — From GPT to ChatGPT

Appendix D ended with a GPT that could generate text. It trained on Shakespeare and learned to produce plausible-sounding continuations. Technically impressive. But ask it "how do I reset my password?" and it'll continue the pattern of your question rather than answer it.

That's the gap. A pre-trained language model is extraordinarily capable at one thing: predicting what text tends to follow other text. What it doesn't know is how to be *helpful*. It doesn't know to answer questions, follow instructions, stay on topic, be honest when it's uncertain, or refuse harmful requests. None of those things are implicit in next-token prediction.

This appendix is about the techniques that close that gap. They're not about architecture — GPT, ChatGPT, Claude, and LLaMA all use the same transformer architecture from Appendix C and D. They're about training: what data you use, what objective you optimize, and how you steer the model's behavior.

---

## What's in this appendix

**Chapter 16 — Fine-Tuning LLMs**

Pre-training gives you a model that knows a lot. Fine-tuning gives you a model that *does* what you want. This chapter covers supervised fine-tuning (SFT) — the step that turns a raw GPT into a model that follows instructions. The key insight: the training loop is identical to pre-training, but the data changes completely. Also: why you have to mask the prompt in the loss, and why getting this wrong wastes training signal.

**Chapter 17 — LoRA: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning**

Fine-tuning a 7B-parameter model with Adam requires keeping ~120GB of stuff in GPU memory. Most people don't have that. LoRA solves it by adding a tiny low-rank side-path to each weight matrix and training only those extra parameters — often 100× fewer than full fine-tuning, with competitive quality. This chapter covers the math, the implementation from scratch, and why initializing B to zeros is non-negotiable.

**Chapter 18 — Alignment: RLHF and DPO**

Instruction-tuned models still hallucinate, produce harmful content, and give confidently wrong answers. Alignment is the attempt to fix this — to train the model to be helpful, honest, and harmless simultaneously. This chapter covers the full RLHF pipeline (SFT → Reward Model → PPO), the Bradley-Terry preference model, PPO's clipping mechanism, and DPO — the 2023 simplification that eliminates the separate reward model while achieving better results.

**Chapter 19 — How ChatGPT Works**

The last piece: how does a fine-tuned, aligned model become a conversational product? Chat templates, system prompts, multi-turn conversation formatting, how the model "sees" the conversation, and what happens under the hood when you send a message to ChatGPT. This chapter ties together everything from the previous ones into a complete picture.
