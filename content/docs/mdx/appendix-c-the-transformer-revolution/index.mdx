# Appendix C — The Transformer Revolution

Appendix B ended with LSTMs hitting a wall. They read one word at a time, so they couldn't use a GPU's parallelism. Seq2seq models squeezed a whole sentence into one fixed-size vector. And gradients still faded over long distances. The best fix anyone had was to bolt attention onto an RNN.

Then in 2017, "Attention Is All You Need" made a radical bet: throw the recurrence away entirely. No RNN, no hidden state carried from step to step — just attention, applied to every position at once.

It worked. Within two years Transformers held nearly every language benchmark; within five they were generating images, predicting protein structures and writing code. Every major language model today — GPT, Claude, Llama, Gemini, Mistral — is a stack of the block this appendix builds.

## What's in this appendix

Every chapter has hand-drawn diagrams, the math written out properly, worked examples you can check by hand, and runnable PyTorch code.

**Chapter 11 — Attention: The Fix for the Bottleneck**

How Bahdanau attention (2015) gave the decoder a fresh, weighted look at every encoder state at each step. The full equations, a worked example, the alignment heatmaps that showed the model learning word order on its own, and the BLEU results. Then Luong's simpler score functions, a small model you can train to watch an alignment appear, how attention shortens gradient paths, and why attention weights aren't explanations.

**Chapter 12 — Self-Attention and Scaled Dot-Product Attention**

A sequence attending to itself. Queries, keys and values as a soft dictionary lookup; $\operatorname{softmax}(QK^{\top}/\sqrt{d_k})\,V$ worked out by hand on a three-token example; the variance argument and softmax gradient behind $\sqrt{d_k}$; causal and padding masks; the $O(n^2)$ cost, and how FlashAttention computes the same thing without storing the $n \times n$ matrix.

**Chapter 13 — Multi-Head Attention**

Why one head isn't enough, and how $h$ heads cost the same as one. The reshape trick that runs every head in a single matrix multiply, $W^O$ as a sum of per-head writes, and what trained heads learn — induction heads, syntactic heads, attention sinks. Then the KV cache, and how multi-query and grouped-query attention shrink it 4–8×.

**Chapter 14 — Positional Encoding**

A proof that attention is blind to word order, then the fixes: sinusoidal encoding and why shifts are rotations, learned positions, relative biases, **RoPE** (the rotation trick inside nearly every modern LLM, with its proof), ALiBi, and how position interpolation, NTK scaling and YaRN stretch models to longer contexts.

**Chapter 15 — Inside a Transformer Block**

Residual connections and the residual stream, LayerNorm vs RMSNorm, why Pre-LN replaced Post-LN, the FFN and SwiGLU, encoder vs decoder and cross-attention. A complete GPT-2 in about forty lines with exactly 124,439,808 parameters, every one accounted for — and how the 2024 Llama 3 block differs from the 2019 one.

By the end of this appendix you'll have built every component from scratch and know exactly what happens to a token on its way through a modern language model.
